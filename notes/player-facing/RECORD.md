# Record

What happened, in play order. Append-only. Never tidied, never summarised down.
Only what came from play goes here.

## Session 1

**Character creation.** Edward Copper — born Uxentio Kupratar, of Kupra in Krysea —
Venturer 1 on the Antiquary template, Lawful, 22 years old. Attributes rolled with
the 5d6 on Charisma and 4d6 on Intellect and Dexterity; Charisma came up 12 and was
floored to 13, Dexterity came up 16, so he ended quicker than he is charming. Hit
points rolled 3, raised to 4 by the minimum. Four hit points.

Lines and veils settled before play: no violence to children, no body horror,
torture veiled. The player was told about the stop/skip/rewind lever.

The Antiquary template was taken by choice rather than rolled, at the player's
request, as a one-time chargen exception.

**The boundary stone on the Mirmen road.** Six days out from Kavala with Destrio
Mennicus's caravan, hired to read boundary stones. The fifth stone of the day was
wrong: it cited eleven leagues to Casra Nuvicerre where Edward's own road notes said
twelve, and though it was of the reign of Ulkyreus like the other four, the chisel
marks were still sharp under a thumb. Edward told Destrio quietly and left the
decision to him. Destrio checked the cut himself, made camp, gave up the ford
crossing, and asked for a written statement a magistrate could read.

**Nauriva left.** Forty minutes into the dig, her mule and her blanket roll were gone
and the road east was empty. She left eleven days' wages on Destrio's board. Destrio
refused to speculate about why. Edward chose not to chase her.

**Under the stone.** Edward finished the dig by firelight. The socket is a separate
block, not granite, and the Auran marker does not fit it — it is wedged plumb with
packed rubble and broken tile that is not old. The block's upper face, sheltered under
the granite's overhang, carries Zaharan carving: an interdiction, not a boundary; one
of a series of at least nine; a house-name worn down to `BET-ASHUR—`. Edward dated it
to roughly 655 BE, the last generation of Zahar. Two markers in one hole, nine hundred
years apart.

**Testing the stone.** Edward went back out with a torch and put the four forgery
tests to it: letterforms, orthography, tooling, patina in the cuts. All four came
back authentic. The inscription is genuinely of the reign of Ulkyreus. What he found
instead was that the inscribed face is *stained* rather than weathered, that a soil
line runs round the shaft three hand-widths above the present ground, and that the
packing wedging the stone plumb is broken roof-tile of Borderlands make, fired within
the last twenty years. He put a piece of the tile in his pocket.

Going back through his own road notes, he found the day's legs ran 1 league, 1¼, 1,
and then **2** — the leg to this stone twice the length of any other that day.

Session ended here: four hours before dawn, the caravan camped a day short of Mirmen
Ford, the written statement signed and saying nothing about any of this.

## Session 2

**Four hours before dawn, at the fifth stone.** Edward went back down with a torch and
looked at the *block* rather than the writing on it. Three things: the ground around the
Zaharan plinth is old, compacted, root-threaded — undisturbed except for the lens of
loose fill holding the granite; the socket cut in the plinth is squarer and larger than
the granite's foot, and its inner edges are weather-rounded, so it stood open and empty
for a long time before the granite came; and the Zaharan text runs around the socket
oriented to be read by someone standing north of it, facing south — by whoever is coming
back down out of the high scrub toward the road.

**Destrio, awake on the tailgate.** He asked the question he'd sat up to ask: at the
crossing, does he pay? Edward said pay. Destrio gave his numbers — three sun, then five,
then six, then eight in Solis; twenty-two sun in a year off four wagons — and committed:
he pays, he smiles, he buys the fodder, and the signed statement goes to the bounds-court
at Casra Nuvicerre when he comes back west in the fifth week, and not before.

**Auran boundary law, which is Edward's trade.** The frontier is a *limes*: a line
surveyed once on the ground and fixed by cut stone; the monuments are the border in law,
the way a deed is not a description of a house. Every stone carries its distance back to
the seat of its survey so a lone stone can still be checked — this one says eleven leagues
to Casra Nuvicerre, and boundary stones point backward, never forward. Behind the stones
is the *forma*, the surveyor's roll, filed at Casra Nuvicerre, where the nearest
bounds-magistrate also sits. Where roll and monument disagree, the roll governs. Somebody
has to produce the roll. Also: moving, defacing or ploughing out a *terminus* is *termini
moti* — loss of the land in dispute, twice its value, and infamy.

**The hole went back.** Two hours with Kest's mattock: spoil in, packing and tile rewedged,
clay trodden, turf laid over. Filled ground still reads as filled ground; the rain came on
at first light and did the rest.

**The story they agreed.** Destrio said flatly he would not lie — can't, is no good at it,
and the man has had four years' practice at being lied to — so the story had to be true:
Edward was distracted by old stones, it cost hours, the hours cost the crossing. Destrio
added the warning: don't say *Zaharan* out loud at that crossing. Say old stones. Old's
enough.

**What Destrio would say about Nauriva, which was facts and no guesses.** Third time he's
hired her, Kavala to Mirmen and back, twice before this year, and both times she came to
him — he never went looking. Best weather-reader he's had and she asks less than she's
worth. And she left the money *counted*: stacks, coppers squared off, eleven days to the
day. "Whatever that is, it isn't running."

**The sixth stone, mid-morning, in the rain.** Edward paced it — double-paces, a bean a
hundred, a mark per ten beans, a thousand double-paces to the mile. Three beans short of
three thousand. The stone stands at a ridge-tail where the road bends: lichened, rounded,
old bird-lime eaten into the shoulder, turf grown tight to the shaft, no packing, weathering
even all the way round, no stain and no soil-line. Same formula, same hand, same reign.
**Thirteen leagues to the marker at Casra Nuvicerre.**

So: the stone behind says eleven, this one says thirteen, by their own figures they stand
two leagues apart, and Edward walked one. The two errors run opposite ways and bracket the
same stone. **One stone. One league. Measured from both sides.** Destrio: "Two stones moved
would've meant a scheme, and schemes have more men in them."

**What Edward knows that makes it matter.** The Ulkyrean survey overshot — the chain of
markers was run the whole way out along this road, past the crossing and beyond, in one
season, for a conquest that never came. So the chain is longer than the Empire and always
has been, and the frontier cannot be "where the stones are." It is *declared*, at a named
stone, in the roll. Every trader on the road knows the saying: the Empire stops about the
fifth stone beyond Casra Nuvicerre. *About.* Nobody has needed to be more precise, because
stones do not walk.

**In sight of the crossing.** Three leagues to Mirmen, half a day in the wet, and the water
up over the stepping-stones before they raise them. From the ridge: the Mirmen running brown
and wide, a stone-built waystation on the near bank with a newer palisade thrown up around
it, and men on the bank in the rain doing something to a rope.
